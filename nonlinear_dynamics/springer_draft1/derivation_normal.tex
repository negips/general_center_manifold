\section{Center Manifold for the Extended System}
\label{sec:center_manifold_derivation}

In the context of invariant manifold theory, several previous approaches for the approximation of such manifolds can be effectively viewed  as instances of what is referred to as the parametrization method. The framework was introduced in a series of papers by \citet{cabre03a,cabre03b,cabre05}, although, a particular instance of such an approach was presented earlier by \citet{coullet83} for the evaluation of the normal form of center manifolds. Nevertheless, subsequent works by \citet*{carini15,jain22,negi24} can be fruitfully viewed through the lens of the parameterization method. Within this framework the reduced representation of the invariant manifold being approximated can be chosen in an arbitrary manner depending on the particular requirements of the reduction being sought. Amongst the plethora of arbitrary choices, the normal form is an important representation that is often sought since it results in the simplest form of the reduced equations. In the next subsection the normal form representation for the center-manifold of the extended system is deduced. 

To simplify notation for multiple indices used hereafter, a multi-dimensional index set $\IndexSet{k}{a,b}$ is introduced, which is an \emph{ordered} set of all  $k$-tuples $(i_{1},i_{2}\ldots,i_{k})$, defined as, 
\begin{equation}
	\label{eqn:index_set}
	\begin{split}
		\IndexSet{k}{a,b} \coloneq &  \bm{\lbrace} (i_{1},i_{2}\ldots,i_{k})\ \bm{|}\ a,b \in \mathds{N}, \\
		& i_{1},i_{2}\ldots,i_{k} \in\mathds{N}, \\
		& a\le i_{i} \le b,\ i_{1}\le i_{2} \le b, \\
		& \ldots, \  i_{k-1}\le i_{k} \le b. \bm{\rbrace},
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
where, $i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}$, are the index variables and $[a,b]$ specify the lower and upper bounds of the indices. Obviously the indices $i_{1}$ \etc\ are dummy indices however their relative position is important since the higher index always starts from the previous index. The set is ordered in the sense that the first element of the $\IndexSet{k}{a,b}$ is always $(i_{1},i_{2}\ldots,i_{k}) = (a,a,\ldots,a)$, the last element is $(i_{1},i_{2}\ldots,i_{k}) = (b,b,\ldots,b)$ and it is the last index $(i_{k})$ which iterates the fastest. Hence the index set $\IndexSet{3}{1,3}$ is the set containing the tuples, 
\begin{alignat}{2}
	(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}) \in\{ & (1,1,1), & (1,1,2), & (1,1,3), \nonumber \\
								  	  & (1,2,2), & (1,2,3),& (1,3,3),  \nonumber \\
								 	  & (2,2,2), & (2,2,3), & (2,3,3), \nonumber \\
								 	  & (3,3,3) \} &,   &   \nonumber 
\end{alignat}
in that order. If an element $(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k})$ preceds an element $(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{k})$ in ordering it is denoted as $(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}) \prec (j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{k})$ while, if  $(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k})$ succeeds $(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{k})$ in the ordering it is denoted as $(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}) \succ (j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{k})$. 

In a similar vein, multiple nested summations are denoted as 
\begin{equation}
	\label{eqn:summation}
	\sum_{(k)}^{[a,b]} = \sum_{i_{1}=a}^{b}\ \sum_{i_{2}=i_{1}}^{b}\ldots\sum_{i_{k}=i_{k-1}}^{b}
\end{equation}
As before, the higher index starts from the previous index. In principle the ordering of the tuples in a particular index set does not matter to the overall results however, keeping the ordering in mind helps to elucidate the structure of the asymptotic problem as will be shown subsequently. 

For the asymptotic expansions we will typically have $a=1$ and $b=m$, where $m$ is the dimension of the manifold. For such cases the number of $k$-tuples in $\IndexSet{k}{1,m}$ is analytically known to be $T^{k}_{m} = C(m+k-1,k)$, where, 
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:binomial}
	C(p,q) = \binom{p}{q} = \dfrac{p!}{q! (p-q)!},
\end{align}
defines the binomial coefficient of $p$ and $q$. 


\subsection{Normal Form}
\label{sec:normal_form}

One may find the exposition on the implementation of the parameterization method in previous works of \citet{haro16} and \citet{jain22}. To be clear, the application of the parameterization method to an extended system like equation~\eqref{eqn:evolution_extended} follows the same steps as outlined in those earlier works. However, many of the essential steps are repeated here to bring out the structure of system of equations at each asymptotic order in the context of system extension.

Consider the extended system as defined by equation~\eqref{eqn:evolution_extended}, represented in a compact form by equation~\eqref{eqn:evolution_extended_compact}, which has an $m$-dimensional critical subspace ($m = n+p+h$). Assume $\vecparv = [\parv_{1}; \parv_{2};\ldots;\parv_{m}]$ is an $m$-dimensional vector of coordinates that parameterize the center-manifold of the extended system. The evolution of the system along the center-manifold in the parameterized coordinates is assumed to be given by 
\begin{align}
	\dt{\vecparv} = \Param(\vecparv), \\
	\Param: \mathds{C}^{m} \rightarrow \mathds{C}^{m} \nonumber.
\end{align}
$\Param$ will also be referred to as the reduced representation (of the extended system). The system solution on the center-manifold corresponding to a point $\vecparv$ of the parameterized system is given by $\CenM(\vecparv)$, which is map from the parameterized coordinates to the full system state,
\begin{align}
	\CenM:\mathds{C}^{m} \rightarrow \mathds{C}^{M}.
\end{align}
 At this stage neither $\Param(\vecparv)$ nor $\CenM(\vecparv)$ have been specified in any form, since, the parameterization can be arbitrarily chosen. In order to obtain a normal form of the reduced system one may expand both $\Param(\vecparv)$ and $\CenM(\vecparv)$ as a formal power series in $\vecparv$ and constrain the asymptotic form of $\Param$ to be in a normal form for the parameterized coordinates $\vecparv$. The asymptotic form of $\CenM$ is obtained by introducing the asymptotic expansions into equation~\eqref{eqn:evolution_extended_compact} and solving the terms order-by-order. 

In what follows, Einstein summation is \emph{not} assumed for repeated indices unless otherwise specified. We assume the power series expansions as,
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:reduced_normal_form}
	\begin{split}
	\Param(\vecparv) =& \sum^{[1,m]}_{(1)}\AsParam_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{1}} + \sum^{[1,m]}_{(2)}\AsParam_{i_{1},i_{2}}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}} + \\
	%
	& \sum^{[1,m]}_{(3)}\AsParam_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}}\parv_{i_{3}} + \ldots,
	\end{split}
\end{align}
and, 
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:asymptotic_normal_form}
	\begin{split}
		\CenM(\vecparv) =& \sum^{[1,m]}_{(1)}\AsECenM_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{1}} + \sum^{[1,m]}_{(2)}\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}} + \\
		%
		& \sum^{[1,m]}_{(3)}\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}}\parv_{i_{3}} + \ldots .
	\end{split}
\end{align}
Here the terms $\AsParam_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},\ldots} \in \mathds{C}^{m}$, while, $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},\ldots} \in \mathds{C}^{M}$. Given the asymptotic form of $\CenM(\vecparv)$, one automatically obtains the nonlinear term $\ENonLin$ as a power series in $\vecparv$. This can be represented as,
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:asymptotic_nonlinear_term}
	\begin{split}
		\ENonLin =& \sum^{[1,m]}_{(2)}\AsEForcing_{i_{1},i_{2}}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}} + \\
		%
		& \sum^{[1,m]}_{(3)}\AsEForcing_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}}\parv_{i_{3}} + \ldots,
	\end{split}
\end{align}
which is obtained via the collection of terms with similar powers. 

Substitution of the power series expansions into equation~\eqref{eqn:evolution_extended_compact} results in 
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:asymptotic_substitution}
	\begin{split}
		\sum^{m}_{\alpha=1}\dfrac{\partial }{\partial \parv_{\alpha}}\Big(\sum^{[1,m]}_{(1)}\AsECenM_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{1}} + \ldots \Big)	 \Big(\sum^{[1,m]}_{(1)}\AsParamEl^{\alpha}_{j_{1}}\parv_{j_{1}} +\ldots \Big)= \\
		%
		\ExtL\Big(\sum^{[1,m]}_{(1)}\AsECenM_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{1}} + \ldots \Big)  +
		% 
		\Big(\sum^{[1,m]}_{(2)}\AsEForcing_{i_{1},i_{2}}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}} + \ldots\Big).
	\end{split}
\end{align}
Here $\AsParamEl^{\alpha}_{j_{1}}$ represents the $\alpha^{th}$ element of the vector $\AsParam_{j_{1}}$. At order $k$, one obtains $m\times T^{k}_{m}$ unknowns which define the elements of the vectors $\AsParam_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}$, and the corresponding $T^{k}_{m}$ unknown vectors $\AsECenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}$. The definitions of the two sets of unknowns are interdependent since the form of the reduced representation $\mathcal{G}(\vecparv)$ determines the map $\CenM(\vecparv)$ from the parameterized coordinates to the full solution (and vice versa). 

\subsubsection{Asymptotic terms of $\mathcal{O}(|\parv|)$}
\label{sec:normal_form_O1}

At first order the non-linear terms do not contribute and one simply obtains,
\begin{align}
		\label{eqn:asymptotic_O1}
		\sum^{[1,m]}_{(1)}\Big( \sum^{m}_{\alpha=1}\AsParamEl^{\alpha}_{i_{1}}\AsECenM_{\alpha} - \ExtL\AsECenM_{i_{1}} \Big)\parv_{i_{1}} = \veczero.
\end{align}
Here, the first restrictions on the asymptotic form of $\Param$ are applied, associating the unknown terms $\AsECenM_{i_{1}}$, $\AsParam_{i_{1}}$ \etc\ with the generalized eigenvalue problem such that,
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eqn:GEV_O1}
	\begin{align}
		\begin{bmatrix} \AsECenM_{i_{1}}, \AsECenM_{i_{2}},\ldots,\AsECenM_{i_{m}}\end{bmatrix} = \EDevMatrix, \\
		\begin{bmatrix} \AsParam_{i_{1}}, \AsParam_{i_{2}},\ldots,\AsParam_{i_{m}}\end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{\widehat{K}}.
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
This reduces equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_O1} to equation~\eqref{eqn:extended_generalized_eigenvalue} which, then satisfies the tangency condition of the invariant manifold, \ie, at the origin the center-manifold is tangent to the center subspace. Subsequently, $\widehat{K}_{i,j}$ will be used for all the first order terms of the type $\AsParam^{i}_{j}$ and the first order vectors $\AsECenM_{i}$ will be replaced by the respective eigenvectors $\EDevec_{i}$. The reduced matrix $\mathbf{\widehat{K}}$ is responsible for the cross-coupling of different $\AsECenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}$ at each order of the asymptotic expansion. 

\subsubsection{Asymptotic terms of $\mathcal{O}(|\parv|^{2})$}
\label{sec:normal_form_O2}

At second order the non-linear terms contribute and one obtains the following equation,
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:asymptotic_O2}
	\begin{split}
		& \sum^{[1,m]}_{(2)}\sum^{m}_{j=1}\Big( \widehat{K}_{i_{1},j}\parv_{i_{2}}\parv_{j} + \widehat{K}_{i_{2},j}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{j} \Big)\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}} + \\
		%
		& \sum^{[1,m]}_{(2)} \sum^{m}_{j=1}\AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},i_{2}}\EDevec_{j}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}} - \sum^{[1,m]}_{(2)} \ExtL\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}} \\ 
		%
		& = \sum^{[1,m]}_{(2)} \AsEForcing_{i_{1},i_{2}}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}}.
	\end{split} 
\end{align}
Collecting the expressions for a particular asymptotic term $\parv_{a}\parv_{b}$, where $(a,b)\in\IndexSet{2}{1,m}$, equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_O2} reduces to,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eqn:asymptotic_O2_ab}
	\begin{split}
		& \Big[ \sum^{a}_{i=1}\widehat{K}_{i,b}\AsECenM_{i,a} +
		\sum^{b}_{i=1}\widehat{K}_{i,a}\AsECenM_{i,b} - \sum^{a}_{i=1}\widehat{K}_{i,b}\AsECenM_{i,a}\delta^{a}_{b} \Big] + \\
		& \Big[
		 \sum^{m}_{i=a}\widehat{K}_{i,b}\AsECenM_{a,i} +
		 \sum^{m}_{i=b}\widehat{K}_{i,a}\AsECenM_{b,i}  - 
		 \sum^{m}_{i=b}\widehat{K}_{i,a}\AsECenM_{b,i}\delta^{a}_{b}\Big] 
		   \\
		& + \sum^{m}_{j=1}\AsParamEl^{j}_{a,b}\EDevec_{j} - \ExtL\AsECenM_{a,b} = \AsEForcing_{a,b}.
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
At second order one obtains $T^{2}_{m}$ equations, one for each tuple $(a,b)\in\IndexSet{2}{1,m}$, which collectively form the system of equations for all $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}$ vectors that needs to be solved. Here, several properties of the structure of equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_O2_ab} need to be pointed out that have important practical implications. 

First, the various unknown vectors $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}$ are coupled due to the off-diagonal terms in $\Khat$, which appear in the first two bracketted terms in equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_O2_ab}. Recall that we are considering the tuples in our index set to be ordered so that the vectors $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}$ can also be ordered in the same manner. For any index $(a,b)\in\IndexSet{2}{1,m}$ (and assuming $b>a$) the bracketted terms in equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_O2_ab} can be rewritten as,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eqn:O2_ab_split}
	\begin{split}
		& \overbrace{\sum^{a}_{i=1}\left(\widehat{K}_{i,b}\AsECenM_{i,a} - \widehat{K}_{i,b}\AsECenM_{i,a}\delta^{a}_{b}\right)}^{P1} +
		\overbrace{\sum^{a-1}_{i=1}\widehat{K}_{i,a}\AsECenM_{i,b}}^{P2} + \\
		  \\
		%
		& \overbrace{\widehat{K}_{a,a}\AsECenM_{a,b}}^{D1} + \overbrace{\sum^{b}_{i=a+1}\widehat{K}_{i,a}\AsECenM_{i,b}}^{S1} + \\
		%
		& \overbrace{\sum^{b-1}_{i=a}\widehat{K}_{i,b}\AsECenM_{a,i}}^{P3} +
		\overbrace{\widehat{K}_{b,b}\AsECenM_{a,b}}^{D2} +
		\overbrace{\sum^{m}_{i=b+1}\widehat{K}_{i,b}\AsECenM_{a,i}}^{S2} + \\
		%
		& \overbrace{\sum^{m}_{i=b}\left(\widehat{K}_{i,a}\AsECenM_{b,i}  - 
		\widehat{K}_{i,a}\AsECenM_{b,i}\delta^{a}_{b}\right)}^{S3}.
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
The terms marked as $P1,P2,P3$ are terms where elements of $\Khat$ multiply vectors $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}$ such that $(i_{1},i_{2})\prec(a,b)$. The terms marked $D1,D2$ are those where elements of $\Khat$ multiply only with $\AsECenM_{a,b}$, and the terms marked as $S1,S2,S3$ are those where $\Khat$ elements multiply vectors succeeding $\AsECenM_{a,b}$, \ie, $(i_{1},i_{2})\succ(a,b)$. Notice that all the succeeding terms in $S1,S2,S3$ are always multiplied by elements of the $\Khat$ matrix that are below the main diagonal. However, since $\Khat$ has an upper triangular structure, all terms marked $S1,S2,S3$ vanish. Therefore the equation resulting from any polynomial term $\parv_{a}\parv_{b}$ couples the vector $\AsECenM_{a,b}$ only with those that preceed it in the ordering, \ie, with $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}\prec\AsECenM_{a,b}$. Thus the resulting system of equations for the $T^{2}_{m}$ unknown vectors $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}$ at second order is lower triangular and even though the fields are coupled with each other, they can still be solved for sequentially one at a time with back substitution. The splitting of terms in equation~\eqref{eqn:O2_ab_split} is done for $b>a$ however, the same conclusion applies even for the case of $b=a$ and the resulting system of equations is always lower triangular. 

A visual representation of this cross coupling is shown in figure~\ref{fig:Khat_coupling_O2}. For a given index $(a,b)\in\IndexSet{2}{1,m}$, the elements of the matrix $\Khat$ marked by $\mathbf{P}$ multiply vectors $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}$ that precede $\AsECenM_{a,b}$ in the ordering. Similarly, the elements marked by $\mathbf{S}$ multiply the vectors $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}$ which succeed $\AsECenM_{a,b}$ in the ordering. The diagonal terms $\widehat{K}_{a,a}$ and $\widehat{K}_{b,b}$ always multiply $\AsECenM_{a,b}$. Obviously, when $\Khat$ is upper triangular, the contributions of the succeeding elements will always vanish.

This lower triangular structure of the resulting system of equations for $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}$ is not confined to the second-order approximation but in fact persists even for higher order asymptotic approximations so that at all orders, the system of equations can always be solved sequentially, one vector at a time. This is an important aspect particularly for large systems since the computational cost of a simultaneous coupled system solve can be prohibitive (as $T^{k}_{m}$ becomes large).

To keep the notation compact, the non-vanishing off-diagonal coupling terms will be represented as $\AsEForcingO_{a,b}$, defined for second order as,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eqn:offdiagonal_O2}
	\AsEForcingO_{a,b} = \left\lbrace
	\begin{aligned}
		\sum^{a}_{i=1}\left(\widehat{K}_{i,b}\AsECenM_{i,a}   
		-\widehat{K}_{i,b}\AsECenM_{i,a}\delta^{a}_{b}\right) \\
		+	\sum^{a-1}_{i=1}\widehat{K}_{i,a}\AsECenM_{i,b} +
		\sum^{b-1}_{i=a}\widehat{K}_{i,b}\AsECenM_{a,i}.
	\end{aligned}\right. 
\end{equation}
The resulting equation for $\AsECenM_{a,b}$ may be written as
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eqn:asymptotic_O2_compact}
	\begin{align}
		\ResolventHat{\omega_{a,b}}^{-1}\AsECenM_{a,b} + \sum^{m}_{i=1}\AsParamEl^{i}_{a,b}\EDevec_{i} 
		 = \AsEForcing_{a,b} - \AsEForcingO_{a,b}, \\
		 %
		\omega_{a,b} = \widehat{K}_{a,a} + \widehat{K}_{b,b},
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where, $\ResolventHatOp(\omega)$ is the resolvent operator for the extended linear system defined as,
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:extended_resolvent}
	\ResolventHatOp(\omega) = (\omega\Ihat - \ExtL)^{-1}.
\end{align}
The resolvent operator is the key mathematical object defining the asymptotic expressions. $\ResolventHatOp(\omega_{a,b})$ is singular when $\omega_{a,b}$ is an eigenvalue of $\ExtL$, which is often referred to as a resonance condition. The normal form of the invariant manifold precisely resolves this singularity by requiring $\AsECenM_{a,b}$ to have a vanishing component along the singular direction. However, equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_O2_ab} still needs to be balanced along the singular directions, which can only be done by $\sum^{m}_{j=1}\AsParamEl^{j}_{a,b}\EDevec_{j}$. Multiplying equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_O2_compact} with $\EAevec_{j}^{\dagger}$ and requiring that $\EAevec_{j}^{\dagger}\AsECenM_{a,b} = 0$ (in case of resonance), then provides us with the definition of $\AsParam_{a,b}$,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eqn:g2_definition}
	\AsParamEl^{j}_{a,b} =
	\begin{cases}
				\EAevec_{j}^{\dagger}(\AsEForcing_{a,b} - \AsEForcingO_{a,b}); & \textit{if}\  \omega_{a,b} = \widehat{K}_{j,j},\\
				0,														& \textit{otherwise},
	\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where, $\EAevec_{j}$ is the $j^{th}$ adjoint eigenvector of $\ExtL$ as defined earlier. Since the system is lower triangular the terms $\AsEForcingO_{a,b}$ are always known apriori when solving the system sequentially.

\subsubsection{Asymptotic terms of $\mathcal{O}(|\parv|^{3})$}
\label{sec:normal_form_O3}

At third (and higher) order one also obtains contributions from the lower order terms.  The sum of all contributions at third order can be written as,
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:asymptotic_O3}
	\begin{split}
		& \sum^{[1,m]}_{(3)}\sum^{m}_{j=1}\left[  
		\widehat{K}_{i_{1},j}\parv_{i_{2}}\parv_{i_{3}}\parv_{j} + 
		%
		\widehat{K}_{i_{2},j}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{3}}\parv_{j} 
		\right. + \\
		%
		& \left.
		\widehat{K}_{i_{3},j}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}}\parv_{j} \right]\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}} +  \\
		%
		& \sum^{[1,m]}_{(2)}\sum^{[1,m]}_{(2)}\left[ 
		\AsParamEl^{i_{1}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}\parv_{i_{2}}\parv_{j_{1}}\parv_{j_{2}} + \AsParamEl^{i_{2}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{j_{1}}\parv_{j_{2}}
		\right]\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}} \\
		%
		%
		& + \sum^{m}_{j=1}\sum^{[1,m]}_{(3)} \AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\EDevec_{j}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}}\parv_{i_{3}}  \\ 
		%
		& - \sum^{[1,m]}_{(3)} \ExtL\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}}\parv_{i_{3}} = \sum^{[1,m]}_{(3)} \AsEForcing_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}}\parv_{i_{3}}.
	\end{split} 
\end{align}
The $\Khat$ matrix is responsible for the cross contributions of different vectors $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}$ as seen from the first set of bracketted terms in equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_O3}. Again, for a given tuple $(a,b,c)\in\IndexSet{3}{1,m}$, the coupling of $\Khat$ elements with vectors preceeding and succeeding $\AsECenM_{a,b,c}$ is depicted in figure~\ref{fig:Khat_coupling_O3}. With $\Khat$ being upper triangular, the contribution of succeeding elements, $(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})\succ (a,b,c)$, vanishes and the resulting system of equations for $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}$ at third order is again lower triangular.

The second set of bracketted terms in equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_O3} are contributions from the lower order asymptotic terms. For a given index $(a,b,c)$, we represent the low order contributions as $\AsEForcingL_{a,b,c}$ and the contributions of the off-diagonal terms as $\AsEForcingO_{a,b,c}$ resulting in the compact expression for $\AsECenM_{a,b,c}$ as
\begin{subequations}
\label{eqn:asymptotic_O3_compact}
\begin{align}
	\begin{aligned}
		\ResolventHat{\omega_{a,b,c}}^{-1}\AsECenM_{a,b,c}  + \sum^{m}_{j=1}\AsParamEl^{j}_{a,b,c}\EDevec_{j} \\ 
		 = \AsEForcing_{a,b,c} - \AsEForcingO_{a,b,c} - \AsEForcingL_{a,b,c},
	\end{aligned}\\
	\omega_{a,b,c} = \widehat{K}_{a,a} + \widehat{K}_{b,b} + \widehat{K}_{c,c}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The resolution of the system singularities due to the resonance condition, $\omega_{a,b,c} = \widehat{K}_{j,j}$, provides us with the third order terms of the reduced representation,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eqn:g3_definition}
	\AsParamEl^{j}_{a,b,c} =
	\begin{cases}
		\begin{split}
			\EAevec_{j}^{\dagger}(\AsEForcing_{a,b,c} - \AsEForcingL_{a,b,c}\\
			 - \AsEForcingO_{a,b,c});
		\end{split}
		 & \begin{split}
			\textit{if},\ \omega_{a,b,c} = \widehat{K}_{j,j}
		\end{split} \\
		0,														& \textit{otherwise},
	\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and $\EAevec^{\dagger}_{j}\AsECenM_{a,b,c} = 0$ if the resonance condition holds. 

\subsubsection{Asymptotic terms of $\mathcal{O}(|\parv|^{k})$}
\label{sec:normal_form_Ok}

For all subsequent orders the structure of the problem remains essentially the same as the third order terms. At any order $k$, one obtains a systems of equations for $T^{k}_{m}$ vectors $\AsECenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}$ which is lower triangular and hence can be solved sequentially. The cross contributions of the vectors due to $\Khat$ has the same structure as in figure~\ref{fig:Khat_coupling_O3} but with $k$ columns (possibly with repetitions) of $\Khat$ contributing. The lower order terms $\AsEForcingL_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}$ are always known a-priori. When solving for the unknown vectors sequentially the off-diagonal contributions $\AsEForcingO_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}$ are also known a-priori. For each index $(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in\IndexSet{k}{1,m}$ one obtains an equation similar to equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_O3_compact}, 
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eqn:asymptotic_Ok_compact}
	\begin{align}
		\begin{aligned}
			\ResolventHat{\omega_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}}^{-1}\AsECenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} + \sum^{m}_{j=1}\AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\EDevec_{j} \\
			 = \AsEForcing_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} - \AsEForcingO_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} 
			 - \AsEForcingL_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}},
		\end{aligned}\\
		%
		\omega_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = \widehat{K}_{i_{1},i_{1}} + \widehat{K}_{i_{2},i_{2}} + \ldots + \widehat{K}_{i_{k},i_{k}},
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
with the resonance condition arising whenever, 
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:resonance_condition_Ok}
	\omega_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = \widehat{K}_{j,j}, && j\in 1,\ldots,m,
\end{align}
which then defines the normal form coefficients,
%\begin{subequations}
%	\label{eqn:resonance_modification_Ok}
%\begin{align}
%	\label{eqn:gk_definition}
%	\AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} =&
%			\EAevec_{j}^{\dagger}(\AsEForcing_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} - \AsEForcingL_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}
%			- \AsEForcingO_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}), \\
%			0 =& \EAevec_{j}^{\dagger}\AsECenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}},
%\end{align}
%\end{subequations}
\begin{equation}
	%\label{eqn:resonance_modification_Ok}
	\label{eqn:gk_definition}
	\AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} =
	\begin{cases}
		\begin{split}
			\EAevec_{j}^{\dagger}(\AsEForcing_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} 
			- \AsEForcingL_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} \\
			- \AsEForcingO_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}})
		\end{split}; & 
		\begin{split}
			\textit{if},\ \omega_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\\
			= \widehat{K}_{j,j}
		\end{split} \\
		 %
		 0 = & \textit{otherwise},
		%0 =& \EAevec_{j}^{\dagger}\AsECenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}},
	\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and, $\EAevec_{j}^{\dagger}\AsECenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = 0$ in case of resonance. 

\subsection{From the Extended System to the Standard System}
\label{sec:extended_to_standard}

For all orders $k\ge2$, one needs to evaluate a resolvent equation of the type given by equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_Ok_compact} for a given right hand side. The $\widehat{\cdot}$ symbol on all terms indicates that the resolvent needs to be evaluated for the extended system. This can potentially be a major drawback since numerical methods for large systems often rely on exploiting the inherent symmetry of the operators which may be destroyed by system extension. However, as it turns out, even though the resolvents are technically solved for the extended linear system $\ExtL$, in fact only the resolvent solutions of the original linear operator $\Lu$ are required. This can be shown by examining the terms sequentially. 

First we express equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_Ok_compact} in its three component form for a generic vector $\AsECenM = [\AsCenM^{(1)}; \AsCenM^{(2)}; \AsCenM^{(3)}]$, 
for a particular frequency $\omega$ and generic right hand side, $\widehat{\mathbf{r}} = [\mathbf{r}^{(1)}; \mathbf{r}^{(2)}; \mathbf{r}^{(3)}]$ as,
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eqn:generic_resolvent}
	\begin{align}
		(\omega\mathbf{I} - \Lu)\AsCenM^{(1)} + \Lnu\AsCenM^{(2)} + \Ltheta\AsCenM^{(3)} = \mathbf{r}^{(1)}, \\
		%
		(\omega\mathbf{I} - \LambdaP)\AsCenM^{(2)} = \mathbf{r}^{(2)}, \\
		%
		(\omega\mathbf{I} - \LambdaH)\AsCenM^{(3)} = \mathbf{r}^{(3)}.
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Clearly, $\mathbf{r}^{(2)}=\veczero$ and an $\omega$ that is non-resonant with $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{P}$ implies $\AsCenM^{(2)} = \veczero$. Similarly, $\mathbf{r}^{(3)}=\veczero$ implies $\AsCenM^{(3)} = \veczero$ when $\omega$ is non-resonant with the external forcing frequencies $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{H}$. In either case, if $\omega$ is resonant with the frequencies in $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{P}$ or $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{H}$, the condition for the removal of singularities, $\EAevec^{\dagger}_{j}\AsECenM = \veczero$,  again ensures $\AsCenM^{(2)} = \veczero$ and $\AsCenM^{(3)} = \veczero$. Therefore equation~\eqref{eqn:generic_resolvent} simply reduces to,
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:generic_resolvent_reduced}
	\Resolvent{\omega}^{-1}\AsCenM^{(1)} = \mathbf{r}^{(1)}; && \ResolventOp({\omega}) = (\omega\mathbf{I} - \Lu)^{-1}, 
\end{align}
where, $\ResolventOp$ is now the resolvent operator for the standard linear system $\Lu$.

The asymptotic terms can now be examined sequentially noting that by construction the extended variables vanish for the non-linear term, which has the form $\ENonLin = [\NewNonLin; \veczero; \veczero]$, and hence so do all $\AsEForcing_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = [\AsForcing_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}};\veczero;\veczero]$ derived from it. Starting at $\mathcal{O}(|\vecparv|^{2})$ and $(i_{1},i_{2})=(1,1)$, one has the resolvent equation in the extended system,
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:asymptotic_O2_11}
		\ResolventHat{\omega_{1,1}}^{-1}\AsECenM_{1,1} + \sum^{m}_{j=1}\AsParamEl^{j}_{1,1}\EDevec_{j} 
		= \AsEForcing_{1,1}
\end{align}
The extended variables vanish for $\AsEForcing_{1,1}$ and therefore, regardless of resonance, they also vanish for $\AsECenM_{1,1}$, which then has the form $ \AsECenM_{1,1} = [\AsCenM_{1,1};\veczero;\veczero]$. Hence equation~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_O2_11} reduces to,
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:asymptotic_O2_11r}
	\Resolvent{\omega_{1,1}}^{-1}\AsCenM_{1,1} + \sum^{n}_{j=1}\AsParamEl^{j}_{1,1}\Devec_{j} 
	= \AsForcing_{1,1},
\end{align}
with $\AsParamEl^{j}_{1,1} = 0$, for $j\in \{n+1,\ldots,m\}$. 

For the next term $(i_{1},i_{2})=(1,2)$, the right hand side now also contains contributions $\hhatbf^{O}_{1,2}$ due to the off-diagonal terms of $\Khat$. These however can only come from the preceeding term, $\AsECenM_{1,1}$, and therefore off-diagonal contributions also have the form $\AsEForcingO_{1,2} = [\AsForcingO_{1,2};\veczero;\veczero]$, which further implies that $\AsECenM_{1,2} = [\AsCenM_{1,2};\veczero;\veczero]$. The resulting equation being, 
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:asymptotic_O2_12r}
	\Resolvent{\omega_{1,2}}^{-1}\AsCenM_{1,2} + \sum^{n}_{j=1}\AsParamEl^{j}_{1,2}\Devec_{j} 
	= \AsForcing_{1,2} - \AsForcingO_{1,2},
\end{align}
with $\AsParamEl^{j}_{1,2} = 0$, for $j\in \{n+1,\ldots,m\}$.

This logic repeats for all $(i_{1},i_{2})\in\IndexSet{2}{1,m}$ and all unknown vectors at second order have the form $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}} = [\AsCenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}; \veczero; \veczero]$ and therefore the resolvent equations can be solved using the original linear system $\Lu$. 

At $\mathcal{O}(|\vecparv|^{3})$ the right hand side also has contributions from $\AsEForcingL_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}$ that arise from the lower order asymptotic terms. However, these arise only due to the second order asymptotic terms $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}$ and therefore, $\AsEForcingL_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}} = [\AsForcingL_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}; \veczero; \veczero]$. The same logical arguments presented earlier apply and hence all third order asymptotic terms also have the form $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}} = [\AsCenM_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}; \veczero; \veczero]$ and only require the standard linear system $\Lu$ for their evaluation. As noted earlier, equations for the higher order asymptotic terms have the same essential structure as the third-order equations and hence at all orders the asymptotic terms have the form $\AsECenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = [\AsCenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}; \veczero; \veczero]$ and only require the standard linear system $\Lu$ for their evaluation. 

Equations~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_Ok_compact} and \eqref{eqn:gk_definition} may be rewritten for the standard system as,
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eqn:asymptotic_Ok_compact_std}
	\begin{align}
		\begin{aligned}
			\Resolvent{\omega_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}}^{-1}\AsCenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} + \sum^{n}_{j=1}\AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\Devec_{j} \\
			= \AsForcing_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} - \AsForcingO_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} 
			- \AsForcingL_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}},
		\end{aligned} \\
		%
		\omega_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = \widehat{K}_{i_{1},i_{1}} + \widehat{K}_{i_{2},i_{2}} + \ldots + \widehat{K}_{i_{k},i_{k}}.
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
with the resonance condition being the same, \ie,
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:resonance_condition_Ok_std}
	\omega_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = \widehat{K}_{j,j}, && j\in 1,\ldots,m,
\end{align}
which then defines the normal form coefficients for $j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$,
%\begin{subequations}
%	\label{eqn:resonance_modification_Ok_std}
%	\begin{align}
%		\AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} =&
%		\Aevec_{j}^{\dagger}(\AsForcing_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} - \AsForcingL_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}
%		- \AsForcingO_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}), \\
%		0 =& \Aevec_{j}^{\dagger}\AsCenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}.
%	\end{align}
%\end{subequations}
\begin{equation}
	\label{eqn:gk_definition2}
	\AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} =
	\begin{cases}
		\begin{split}
			\Aevec_{j}^{\dagger}(\AsForcing_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} 
			- \AsForcingL_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} \\
			- \AsForcingO_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}})
		\end{split}; & 
		\begin{split}
			\textit{if},\ \omega_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\\
			= \widehat{K}_{j,j}
		\end{split} \\
		%
		0 = & \textit{otherwise},
	\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and $\Aevec^{\dagger}_{j}\AsCenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = 0$ in case of resonance. 
For all $j\in\{n+1,\ldots,m\}$, 
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:resonance_modification2_Ok_std}
	\AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} =&	0,
\end{align}
regardless of resonance.

The entire procedure for the evaluation of the normal form is presented succinctly in algorithm~\ref{algo:normal_form}. 
 \begin{algorithm}
	\caption{Center--Manifold: Normal form}\label{algo:normal_form}
	\begin{algorithmic}[0]
		\Require System: $\partial \vecu/\partial t = \Lu \vecu + \NonLin(\vecu,\vecMu)$.
		\Ensure $\NonLin(\veczero,\vecMu) = \veczero;$ $\partial\NonLin/\partial \vecu = \veczero$. 
		\Require Tangent Space: $\AevMatrix^{\dagger}\Lu \DevMatrix = \LambdaC$.
		\LComment{Center subspace dimension: $n$.} 
		\Require Extension via $\vecnu, \vectheta$ and $\NewNonLin(\vecu,\vecnu,\vectheta)$.
		\State Evaluate $\EDevMatrix,\Khat$ via algorithm~\ref{algo:extended_direct_tangent}. 
		\LComment{Center subspace dim.: $m = n + p + h$.} 
		\Require Maximum Asymptotic order: $k_{max}$
		\LComment{Substitute asymptotic expansions}
		\For{$(k\in 2,3,\ldots,k_{max})$}
			\For{$(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in \IndexSet{k}{1,m}$}
				\State \LComment {Build all r.h.s. contributions.}
				\State $\mathbf{r} = \AsForcing_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} - \AsForcingO_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} - \AsForcingL_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}$ %\Comment{Resolvent r.h.s} 
				\State $\omega = \widehat{K}_{i_{1},i_{1}} + \widehat{K}_{i_{2},i_{2}} + \ldots + \widehat{K}_{i_{k},i_{k}}$
				\State \LComment {Build $\AsParam_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}$, Eq.~\eqref{eqn:gk_definition2}}
				\For{$(j \in 1,\ldots,n)$}
					\If {($\omega = \widehat{K}_{j,j}$)} 
						\State $\AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = \Aevec^{\dagger}_{j}\mathbf{r}$
					\Else
						\State $\AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = 0$
					\EndIf
				\EndFor
				\For{$(j \in n+1,\ldots,m)$}
					\State $\AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = 0$
				\EndFor
				\State \LComment {Build singular components}
				\State $\mathbf{r}_{s} = \sum_{j=1}^{n}\Devec_{j}\AsParamEl^{j}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}$ 
				\State \LComment {Evaluate resolvent}
				\State $\AsCenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = \ResolventOp(\omega)[\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_{s}]$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eqn:asymptotic_Ok_compact_std}}
				\LComment {$\ResolventOp(\omega) = (\omega\mathbf{I} - \Lu)^{-1}$}
				\State $\AsECenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}} = [\AsCenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}; \veczero; \veczero]$
			\EndFor
		\EndFor \\
		\Return $\Param(\vecparv), \CenM(\vecparv)$.
	\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The fact that the extended variables vanish for all asymptotic vectors $\AsECenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}$ with $k\ge2$ has another simplifying consequence for the parameter perturbations $\vecnu$ and harmonic forcing amplitudes $\vectheta$. Since these variables have been included into the state vector $\evecu$, asymptotic evaluation of the state vector means that these extended variables are also evaluated asymptotically. Therefore,
\begin{align}
	\nu_{j} = \sum_{(1)}^{m}(\AsECenM_{i_{1}})_{N+j}\parv_{i_{1}} + \sum_{(2)}^{m}(\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}})_{N+j}\parv_{i_{1}}\parv_{i_{2}} + \ldots,
\end{align}
where, $(\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}})_{N+j}$ represents the $(N+j)^{th}$ component of the vector $\AsECenM_{i_{1},i_{2}}$. The high order vectors $\AsECenM_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}$ with $k\ge2$ do not contribute while, for the linear term, $(k=1)$, only one eigenvector has a non-vanishing contribution to $\nu_{j}$. Therefore we have a simple relation between the parameter perturbations $\nu_{j}$ and the reduced coordinates $\vecparv$ as,
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:parameter_reduced_representation}
	\nu_{j} = \parv_{n+j}.
\end{align}
In an identical manner, the relation between the forcing mode variables $\theta_{j}$ and the reduced coordinates is simply,
\begin{align}
	\label{eqn:theta_reduced_representation}
	\theta_{j} = \parv_{n+p+j}.
\end{align}
